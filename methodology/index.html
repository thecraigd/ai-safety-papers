Methodology

How The Guardrail finds, summarizes and ranks AI safety research, and what it cannot do. Every step is done by language models: no one selects, edits or reorders papers by hand.

The daily pipeline

The pipeline runs every day from 06:00 UTC. A run can take several hours, because it works within a daily budget of model usage; papers the budget does not reach are processed on later days.

  1. Fetch. New submissions are collected from the arXiv API in cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.CL, cs.CV, cs.NE, stat.ML, cs.CR and cs.MA.
  2. Screen. GPT-5.6 Terra reads each paper's title and abstract and estimates how relevant it is to AI safety, from 0 to 1. Papers at 60% or above enter the index. So far 38% of screened papers have.
  3. Categorize and tag. The same model assigns one or more of six categories and tags from a fixed list of 51.
  4. Summarize. It writes a one- or two-sentence summary of the paper's main contribution.
  5. Score. GPT-5.6 Terra scores the paper from 1 to 10 on five criteria (below).
  6. Review. Each day's strongest candidates (up to 25) get a second, more careful review by GPT-6 Sol, a stronger model, which scores them again on the same criteria.

Every step uses only the title and abstract. Model output is published as it is; summaries that come back cut off are rewritten by the model later, not by hand.

How papers are scored

Each paper is scored from 1 to 10 on five criteria. The weighted score, out of 10, combines them:

Criterion Weight The question the model answers
Significance 35% How important is this for AI safety? Does it address a critical problem or open a new direction?
Novelty 25% How original is the contribution? Does it introduce new ideas or significantly advance existing ones?
Technical quality 20% How rigorous is the method? Are the claims supported by the evidence described?
Timeliness 15% How relevant is it to current AI development, emerging risks or capabilities?
Accessibility 5% How readable is it for a broad machine-learning audience? Can practitioners use it?

weighted score = significance × 0.35 + novelty × 0.25 + technical quality × 0.20 + timeliness × 0.15 + accessibility × 0.05

The first-pass model and the review model score on noticeably different scales, so their numbers should not be compared with each other. On this site the score beside a paper in a ranked list is always the one that put it there: the review score where there is one. Browse sorts and filters by the first-pass score, which every paper has, and shows the review score alongside where it exists.

The Daily Five and The Weekly Ten

The Daily Five

The five highest-ranked papers added on each day: papers with a second-stage review come first, ordered by their review score, followed by any others by first-pass score. See The Daily Five.

The Weekly Ten

Each week's list is made automatically on Sundays at 23:00 UTC, from every paper added that week. The strongest candidates (up to 40, balanced across the days of the week) are ranked by their second-stage review scores, and the top ten are published. The review model also writes the week's overview. The list is sent by email on Mondays. See The Weekly Ten.

Categories and tags

Each paper is assigned to one or more of six broad categories:

Interpretability
Understanding how models work internally: circuits, features, representations and mechanistic analysis.
Alignment
Training AI to do what we intend: RLHF, preference learning, value alignment, reward modeling and goal specification.
Robustness & Security
Adversarial attacks, jailbreaks, prompt injection, defenses, content filtering and guardrails.
Evaluations
Measuring AI capabilities or safety: benchmarks, red-teaming, safety evaluations and capability assessments.
Agents & Control
Autonomous AI systems, tool use, human oversight, controllability, corrigibility and multi-agent safety.
Policy & Governance
AI regulation, deployment practices, societal impact, risk assessment, position papers and policy proposals.

Papers also get several tags from a fixed list of 51 narrower topics, such as Jailbreaking, Sparse Autoencoders, Reward Hacking, Red Teaming, Tool Use, Corrigibility, Deception and Biosecurity. The model can propose new tags, but the list only changes when a person approves one. Papers are never tagged, scored or ranked by hand.

Limitations

  • Titles and abstracts only. The models do not read the full papers, so they can miss important details, caveats or flaws.
  • Model-written text can be wrong. Summaries, "why it matters" lines, overviews and scores are generated by language models and may contain errors or miss the main point.
  • Imperfect screening. Some relevant papers will be missed and some less relevant ones included.
  • Scores are estimates, not peer review. They reflect a model's judgement of importance and quality from an abstract. Read a paper yourself before relying on it or citing it.
  • Possible bias. Language-model judgements can reflect biases from the models' training, for example towards familiar topics or writing styles. We have not measured this.
  • Delays. Papers usually appear a day or two after they are announced on arXiv, and later when a day's volume exceeds the processing budget.
  • The method changes. Models and ranking methods have changed over time (below), so scores from different periods are not directly comparable.

Spotted a mistake, or a paper that should or should not be here? Please get in touch.

Changes to the method

  1. December 2025

    The Guardrail starts. Google Gemini Flash models screen, summarize and score new arXiv papers in a single stage.

  2. January 2026

    The first weekly list of the top ten papers, then called "Editor's Choice", for the week of 22–28 December 2025.

  3. May 2026

    Screening moves to a lighter Gemini model (Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite) to control running costs.

  4. August 2026

    Screening, summaries and scores move to OpenAI GPT-5.6 Luna.

  5. 24 September 2026

    Two-stage review. GPT-5.6 Terra screens, summarizes and scores every paper; GPT-6 Sol re-reviews the strongest candidates, and its scores decide the daily and weekly rankings. Scores before and after this change are on different scales. (For one day before this, 23 September, GPT-6 Luna did the screening.)

  6. October 2026

    "Editor's Choice" is renamed The Weekly Ten and "Top Daily Picks" The Daily Five, to make clear that language models, not an editor, choose them. Ranked lists now show the score that ranked them.

Currently in use, as recorded on the latest papers: GPT-5.6 Terra for first-pass scores and GPT-6 Sol for second-stage reviews.

Sources and attribution

Paper metadata and abstracts come from arXiv through its API, under arXiv's API terms of use. Thank you to arXiv for use of its open access interoperability. The Guardrail is not affiliated with or endorsed by arXiv, and individual papers may carry their own licenses.

The index also includes 2,873 accepted papers from ICLR and NeurIPS (2025 and 2026), collected from OpenReview and screened the same way. This set is collected occasionally, not daily.

Open data

The whole index is free to download and use in your own research, analysis or tools:

  • all-papers.json: every arXiv paper with its abstract, summary, categories, tags, scores and the models' brief reasoning (JSON, a large file of over 100 MB).
  • browse-index.json: a smaller version without abstracts or reasoning, including conference papers (JSON).

Both are updated daily. Download them as often as is useful; the terms set out what you can do with them. If you build something with them, we would like to hear about it.
